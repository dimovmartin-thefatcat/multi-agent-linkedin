### Example 2 — *(format: long_article)*

```
4 ways in which ChatGPT will change the customer service and customer analytics industry
Martin Dimov
GenAI and ML practitioner

March 6, 2023
'It’s the end of the world as we know it! Nothing will be the same!’, exclaimed a colleague of mine on one of our regular Teams meetings, provoking a skeptic smirk on my face. The date was December 3rd and the most renowned ‘child’ of AI was only 3-days old. Since then, a lot of things have happened and my skepticism has been perishing slowly.

For once, the latest stats on ChatGPT show 13 mln. people have been using it on а daily basis in January 2023 alone. In February OpenAI declared surpassing the 100 mln. subscribers benchmark and made Google rankings as the most popular topic on the Internet at the moment.

Many have speculated about the potential outcomes and impacts on various industries and fields of expertise. Some remain skeptical (like me) patiently waiting for more details and more shared practices, other jumped right into the deep waters and started making business plans and even developing new products. It was widely speculated that that new technology will be a game changer for many industries and businesses. 

So, here’s my take of how ChatGPT can improve management of customer experience and customer service:

Better chabots and automated customer service – a lot of companies introduced chatbots to save on customer service expenses, but they are still imperfect. Their shortcomings when it comes to understanding natural written human expressions provide an inexhaustible fuel for memes and jokes on the Internet. ChatGPT’s powerful engine has the potential to drastically increase the reliability, accessibility and continuity of chatbots and help them provide more adequate service. They will be able to detect and solve problems in a quicker way and prevent escalations by customers leading to loss of revenue. Although I don’t expect a completely “humanless” service in the next 4-5 years, organisations with big customer service departments and the adjacent service industry will have to adjust to meet the inevitably raised standards.
Text manipulation and technical maintenance of customer complaints and interactions – More optimal and more automated customer service through optimized communications would lead to easier and seamless ways for data collection, data manipulation and eventually faster and more reliable data analyses. Providing a proper data base for that and many more analyses is an important step that requires special optimization, automation, and sustainability. Social media content generated by customers has exploded in the past decade. More than one in every five people prefer using social media and mobile apps as a preferred channel of communication with Customer Service. That creates an enormous amount of data in the form of unsolicited text, video and images, which is also a great data source for further analytics. ChatGPT’s main advantages are reaching far from just text generation but include text manipulation as well, which more analysts and technical people would be most happy about. Machine translation and indexed approach to unstructured text are just but the few features that not only will increase productivity and efficacy of the people engaged with manually skimming vast amount of text to draw conclusions but also executives that struggle with time and knowledge to do so.
Predictive analytics – ChatGPT application in real life will solve some of the main obstacles in terms of finding good analytical tools, setting, adjustments and maintaining elaborate environments and use of powerful machines to run the entire analytical process. One of the most emblematic features of the algorithm is summarizing large amounts of raw text in predefined format by distilling the essence of individual content. That would mean a direct implication on the NLP methods in the area of topic generation and text summarization. A useful feature may include automatic insight generations for automated and even industrialized analytical approach based on various parameters and variables directly fed into the algorithm. The technology would allow real time data processing and summarization of the main problems customers discuss or share in their interactions with Customer Service Agents, chatbots, social networks or even face to face on-the-spot contacts with sales representatives. The latest developments and ever-expanding capabilities behind GPT models also claim a better and more accurate Sentiment Analyses, which is always good news not only for the analysts, but for C-level people and CRM professionals providing new dimensions and more zoomed and timely approach on particular situation, specific cases or even escalations.
Improved Customer Segmentation through impersonating different roles – customer segmentation plays a vital role in every CX analysis. It defines main personas in and defines the targeted customer groups by assuming similarities within and difference among them. One of the most evident challenges all analysts have been facing until now, was the fact that every segmentation is more or less a stationary snapshot of the customer ‘status quo’. The GPT engines can provide additional layers of analytics and summarization by quickly distinguishing customers and even further expand and predict the features of a certain proxy profile behaviour. This, potentially could be done in real time and can adapt to every sudden or ongoing change in customer status, thus constantly monitoring any shifts in customer responses and signaling for respective actions, personalized marketing campaigns and possibly further actions to increase retention.

A word of caution though. Despite the numerous benefits of the GPT-3 technology, the outputs it generates might not always be the most accurate and verified. Some sources suggest huge discrepancies and even false information included in the final insights generated by the engine. There could be two main reasons for that. The first is the fact that it’s a tool that predicts the next most appropriate word in the context of the words or questions provided so far. So, when you write a text or a sentence the algorithm searches for solution of what would be the next word based on the information the model was trained at, which typically includes millions of pages of web content. That on its part may include false and unverified facts, deliberate misinformation, propaganda etc. Since there is no integrated filter on the input data itself, that would mean creating a layer of falsified training data which may be ingested and included in the prediction itself.

The second reason lay in the architecture of the algorithm itself. The predicted array of words may not necessarily mean the most probable (the one with highest predicted probability) is chosen as next. The ‘magical’ part here is the selection of words and expression so that the text would increase its creativity and the ambiance of the final output. All this is controlled by a parameter called ‘temperature’ which tweaks how creative or straight to the point the algorithm response should be. Hence, it is up for the engineers behind the model to manually give a value for this parameter. In these cases, the overall accuracy may suffer of completely go off-track for the sake of increased creativity or artistic value of the outputs.

Despite these shortcomings, the application the GPT-3 is in fact a major leap in the overall machine learning evolution. In the past we used to measure the development of computer technology in 'frog' and 'tiger' leaps, whereas now we are forced to assume drastically bigger measurements. The GPT leap is not only relevant to its own development, but it will also force other algorithms to grow just as fast and as accurate as GPT which in its terms is a major development itself.

Only time will tell if my colleague was right to begin with, but what is certain that trends like this would act as a booster for the overall development of CX analytics, predictive power of models involved in summarizing customer generated content and predictive analytics as a whole. That would help companies to better detect problems, act on it and build more effective strategies aimed at the specific needs of their customers.
```
